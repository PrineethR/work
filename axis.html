Axis Bank

Axis Design Lab, Bengaluru
July 2023 — December 2024

Role Product designer
Verticals Five
Sound touchpoints 7+
Principles Participatory · Personal

Project

A year and a half at India's third-largest private sector bank, inside a team that runs as a hybrid of studio and corporate function. My role spanned five verticals — — which meant working with business, marketing, engineering and compliance in roughly equal measure. For confidentiality, the actual visuals and exact metric values are omitted here.

The starting problem was a brand perceived as premium that needed to become approachable. An audit across product verticals found illustrations, photography and 3D being used for widely different contexts, and video from vendors diverging from video made internally. Colour turned out to be the most recognisable element of the brand — and props and characters the most dangerous, easily read as cartoonish, and so not serious.

The framework we built separates : what must stay fixed for recognition, and what can flex per product. On top of that sat implementation guidelines for illustration, motion and 3D, a component-based illustration system, motion principles that survive low-end devices, and colour systems meeting WCAG AA.

The absence of sound was the gap I went looking for. Sixteen interviews on emotional response to financial interactions found that a large number of people carry real anxiety about whether a transaction actually went through. That gave the work its first target: . The architecture is hierarchical — primary, secondary, tertiary — with signature elements for recognition and functional sounds for feedback, built with sound designers, musicians and audio engineers, and rolled out in three phases.

Olympus was a financial literacy game that had been designed over three years and then shelved. I took it out of the drawer. It needed an overhaul mechanically, aesthetically and dynamically: a rebuilt acquisition flow, a learning path that balances entertainment against educational outcome, and a progressive difficulty system that adapts to how a player is actually doing.

The harder, slower work was advocacy. Teams outside design did not know an illustration and motion system existed, or that there was a team maintaining it. I designed a workshop series and role-specific material connecting design principles to what each function actually does, with a measurement framework tracking adoption and turnaround time. Alongside it, the was rewritten as a narrative about how design systems work between designers and developers, rather than as documentation that was already functionally fine and still unpersuasive.

What I take from it: advocating for design across functions is harder than I thought, and evidence moves people where enthusiasm does not. Oscillating between zooming out to strategy and zooming into pixels is genuinely exhausting. And handling projects end to end is where I work best.
