A Living Painting

University of the Arts Utrecht
Exchange programme, 2023

Role Solo — concept to build
Medium Projection mapping
Viewing time 18 s → 3.2 min
Principles Playful · Poetic

Project

An interactive projection mapping installation that turns Van Gogh's Starry Night into something that responds to where you are standing and how you move. Built solo during my exchange at the University of the Arts Utrecht. Median viewing time went from eighteen seconds to three minutes and twelve.

It started from a question I was slightly embarrassed to ask: It turned out plenty of people shared the sentiment. Research with 17 participants, observation of 32 gallery visitors and surveys of 45 art and non-art students converged on four barriers — a knowledge gap around technique and context, a passive format that conflicts with interactive expectations, physical distance that reduces intimacy, and a fixed perspective that leaves nothing to discover.

The build is a TouchDesigner patch driving displacement, blur and feedback across two video sources, with as the input. The constraint I set myself was that the painting had to stay recognisably the painting: the viewer changes it, but does not replace it.

Proximity does the real work. Up close you get technique; from further back you get context — which produced three times more physical exploration of the viewing space. Motion-triggered layers surfaced details people had looked past: "I never noticed these details in the original painting." Elements that needed more than one viewer to trigger generated unprompted conversations about technique between strangers.

The line I keep is from a visitor: "for the first time, I feel like I understand why his brushwork matters." I pitched the concept to the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam, where the conversation turned — correctly — to artistic integrity, conservation, visitor flow and how you would ever measure impact.

Documentation frames from the running installation. The original painting appears as research context.
